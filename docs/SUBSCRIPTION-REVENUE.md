# Subscription revenue reports

Open **Reports → Revenue** for the selected newsletter. The dashboard, REST and MCP read the same report. Opening it reads only stored data. **Refresh known subscriptions** explicitly reads up to 20 known contacts' subscriptions from the creator's own Stripe account, with a continuation cursor; it also reads at most each latest invoice and a bounded set of discount terms. It changes no charge, subscription, reader access or email delivery. It is not an enumeration or historical import of the whole Stripe account.

## What each number means

All amounts remain in their original currency. Different currencies never become one total. The API and CSV use the currency's smallest unit; calculated monthly normalization can have fractional units. The dashboard uses the currency's normal display precision, including zero-decimal currencies.

- **Paid invoice snapshots** sum Stripe's recorded `amount_paid`, grouped by the invoice's `paid_at` in UTC. This can include customer credit and invoices marked paid outside Stripe. It is not cash received, recognized revenue, net revenue or a payout. Refunds, disputes, processing fees and payouts are not deducted. Invoice totals excluding tax and post-payment credit-note amounts are shown separately when Stripe supplies them. A paid invoice with a recorded zero amount is zero; a missing amount is unknown.
- **Calculated MRR** normalizes the latest observed fixed recurring Stripe price and quantity into a month. It includes `active` and `past_due`, excludes legacy trials, individual items in a current trial, metered items, one-time items and fees, free prices and paused collection. It is a calculation from observed contract facts, not a payable invoice or guaranteed payment. It does not use today's newsletter tier list price. Comps, gifts, lifetime purchases and other one-time sales do not become recurring revenue.
- **Recurring discounts** are subtracted in their recorded order when their duration, amount and product scope are known. One-time discounts are excluded by this report's explicit MRR policy. Expired discounts do not reduce MRR. Unsupported or hidden discount terms, transformed/tiered prices, mixed currencies or ambiguous tax adjustments leave net MRR unknown. A fixed subscription-level discount across multiple items is left unknown rather than allocating it by guess. Tax-inclusive prices may have a known monthly price value but unknown net MRR.
- **Monthly price value before adjustments** is the observed fixed Stripe price/quantity normalization before discount or tax adjustments. It is not net MRR. Known subtotals remain distinct from totals with missing data.
- **Observed changes** compare two stored provider observations. A first observation is not a newly acquired paid reader. Tier upgrades/downgrades need two exact tier IDs and an unchanged recorded tier order; reordering or deleting tiers does not prove a reader upgraded. Activation, reactivation, monetary expansion/contraction, cancellation/unpaid, pause and currency changes are separate observations.
- **Tracked cohort loss** follows subscriptions observed before the start of the selected window that then had a positive recurring price. Its baseline is the state before the first change in the window, or the unchanged latest state. It counts those ending canceled or unpaid; a reactivation can make the ending loss zero while a cancellation event remains counted. This is a tracked cohort, not a whole-business churn rate. Unknown baselines, no starting cohort or incomplete scans leave the rate blank.

The existing `get_paid_report` keeps its earlier, explicitly labelled tier list-price estimate. This separate report provides observed subscription/invoice facts and calculated MRR, without changing that API contract.

## Coverage and refresh

History is always labelled incomplete: earlier invoices and transitions are not automatically backfilled. A latest invoice read during refresh may predate when recording began. The report shows observation dates and known individual and group subscriptions with no snapshot. A dash means unknown; it never fills missing history with zero business revenue. Old snapshots remain old until another verified event or explicit refresh observes new state.

Subscription events read current Stripe state before recording, so a stale event body does not restore an old subscription state. Price observations use a request-start watermark and compare-and-swap revision. Financial history uses a small pending journal in the same atomic state write; unique change keys materialize it once, and read-only reports merge pending changes so an interrupted history write is neither lost nor counted twice. Invoice source watermarks refuse older snapshots and reductions of already observed paid amounts.

Financial capture from existing verified webhooks and applied imports is best effort and cannot break membership access. Failed observations can be repaired for current state by explicit refresh; this does not reconstruct intermediate transitions that were never observed. Recording stops for an account awaiting deletion, including a caller holding an older account object.

Refresh validates FlockStack metadata or an existing import mapping before additional discount reads and after an expanded subscription read. It uses no new global Stripe API version. Supported modern discount IDs are expanded, then coupon facts are read with explicit product/currency scope. At most ten coupon reads are made per refresh page; unsupported account-version expansion leaves the amount unknown. Provider reads use five-second timeouts and a 45-second loop budget, with a cursor to continue unfinished pages. The response separately counts observed subscriptions, skipped reads and invoices recorded; a failed invoice lookup can leave the subscription snapshot updated. A later unexpanded webhook observation can again leave discount-derived MRR unknown until refresh resolves those terms.

The report scans at most 5,000 subscription snapshots, 20,000 changes, 10,000 invoice snapshots and 5,000 known-contact and 5,000 group references. It identifies an incomplete scan and leaves full totals/rates unavailable. Counts and known subtotals then describe the scanned records. Monthly tables contain observed invoices only; unobserved months are not filled with zero. The private account export streams all stored records for independent reconciliation.

## API, storage and privacy

- `get_subscription_revenue_report`: read, rolling 1–730 days, default 30.
- `export_subscription_revenue_report`: read, CSV of the same report, including basis and coverage; unknown amounts are blank.
- `refresh_subscription_revenue`: write, external read, 120 calls per account per hour; existing key presets, newsletter scopes, daily caps and team permissions apply. Refresh is available while blocked because it changes only report snapshots, and remains unavailable during account deletion.

The three account-scoped models store normalized status, prices, tier references, financial changes, amounts/currencies, Stripe subscription/invoice/event IDs and source/observation dates. They do not store customer names, addresses, card details, email addresses, coupon codes/names, or whole Stripe objects. Records stay until account deletion, including after a reader contact is removed. Private exports include `subscription-financials.csv`, `subscription-changes.csv` and `subscription-invoices.csv`; account purge removes all three. No financial data is shared between regions.

Migration `0039_subscription_revenue.sql` creates their tables/indexes and a partial Contact lookup index. It remains a local, unapplied production migration. No new provider, Worker or recurring job is needed. All build-time billing checks use synthetic Stripe fixtures; they do not prove a live account's event configuration or API-version behavior.

Definitions were checked against [Stripe Billing analytics](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/analytics), the [Subscription object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/subscriptions/object), the [Invoice object](https://docs.stripe.com/api/invoices/object), [discount expansion](https://docs.stripe.com/api/discounts/object) and [coupon retrieval](https://docs.stripe.com/api/coupons/retrieve). Stripe Dashboard definitions can be configured differently; reconcile these observed reports with Stripe rather than treating them as an accounting ledger.

Group subscription observations use the same currency-separated financial model. A group is one recurring subscription with its actual licensed quantity, not one subscription per invited reader. Refresh follows contact pages, then group pages (the returned cursor marks the phase). Groups can also explicitly refresh billing on their management screen; that read updates funded access and captures the financial snapshot. The report-only refresh changes no access or billing. Group buyers are never enrolled by financial observation.
