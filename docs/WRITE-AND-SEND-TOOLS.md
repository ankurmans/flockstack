# Write and send tools

Small additions to writing and sending an issue. Each works on FlockStack Cloud and on a self-hosted server, and the ones with an action are in the registry, so the dashboard, the REST API and MCP have them. None adds a table, an index or a migration: the new fields are in the newsletter and campaign documents.

## Share buttons in the email header

A newsletter can add X, LinkedIn, Facebook and email links beside "Read online" at the top of its emails (`shareButtons` on `update_newsletter`, or the Share buttons switch in the newsletter's settings). An issue can say otherwise for itself: `shareButtons` on `create_campaign` and `update_campaign` is true, false, or null to follow the newsletter, and the issue's Share row in the send step does the same. The links share the issue's web page (and its subject), so an issue with no public page (website off, unlisted, paid, or sent by email only) has none, and neither do emails about what a reader did (an order) or the web version itself. They are plain links: nothing is loaded from the networks. Like any link in an issue they are click tracked, and UTM tagging leaves them alone. The label stays in English, like "Read online". `lib/share-links.js` has the addresses.

## Custom email headers

`customHeaders` on `update_newsletter` (and the Custom headers box in settings, one `Name: value` a line) adds up to 10 headers to the newsletter's emails. Only these are accepted: names starting with `X-` (not `X-SES-`, `X-Amz-`, `X-PostShiba`, `X-Feedback` or `X-FlockStack`), and `List-Id`, `List-Archive`, `List-Help`, `List-Owner`, `List-Subscribe`, `Organization` and `Keywords`. A value is up to 200 characters of printable ASCII on one line, so a line break can't start another header. Everything else, including From, Reply-To, Subject, Message-ID, DKIM-Signature, Feedback-ID, List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post, is refused when saved, filtered again when an email is built, and written over by the real headers when a name matches. They are per newsletter, not per list, template or issue. `lib/custom-headers.js` has the rules.

## Images in the editor

**Size and crop.** A picture selected in the editor shows a small menu: Small, Medium, Large and Full width set its width (240, 360, 480 and 600 pixels, the email's width) and keep its shape, besides dragging its sides; Crop opens a box to drag, free or in a 1:1, 4:3, 3:2 or 16:9 shape. The cropped part is saved as a new picture in the media library and takes the old one's place in the issue (one undo step); the original stays where it was uploaded. A GIF can be resized but not cropped, which would end its animation. The crop reads the picture's pixels in the browser: an account's own uploads come through `/api/uploads/source` (the dashboard-only `read_image_source` action, which only reads an address the account uploaded), and any other picture is read directly, which works where its host allows it. `lib/image-edit.js` has the arithmetic.

**GIFs.** With `GIPHY_API_KEY` set, the image picker has a GIFs tab and `search_gifs` (REST `GET /gifs`) searches GIPHY, G rated, and answers GIFs with the `html` for a rich campaign. Without a key the tab is hidden and the action answers `unavailable`; nothing else needs the key. README, GIFs, has GIPHY's rules and what leaves the server. GIPHY is listed in `lib/subprocessors.js` as an optional service that FlockStack Cloud does not use.

## Random cohort sends

`send_campaign` and `schedule_campaign` take `audienceOrder`: `engaged` (the default, the most engaged first) or `random`. With `audienceLimit` a random send reaches a random cohort of that many contacts, which is also a fair sample for a first look at results (the dashboard's Limit row has "Most engaged first" and "Random cohort"). Random means a fixed shuffle for the campaign: each contact's rank is a hash of the campaign's ID and its own (`lib/cohort-order.js`), so engagement, sign-up date and address play no part, a different campaign takes a different cohort, and the same campaign always sees the same order. That is what makes it safe to repeat: counting a send, queueing it again after a failure and each `send_campaign_to_more` step (which keeps the campaign's order) take a longer prefix of the same order, and the one-email-per-contact index skips whoever already got the issue. A contact who joins later may fall inside a cohort that hasn't gone out yet; one who already got the issue is never sent it twice. The list cleanup counts issue recipients inside the cohort as it does for the most engaged. The audience's ids are ranked in memory, so a random cohort is refused for an audience over 500,000 contacts (send to a segment instead). A subject line test's slice also follows the order. `preview_campaign_recipients` and `preview_campaign_cleanup` take `audienceOrder` to count either way; the recipients preview's sample still lists the most engaged. Percent isn't a separate setting: choose the count.

## Campaign list: tags and archive

`list_campaigns` takes `tag` (a tag's name or its web address form) and `archived`, and the dashboard's campaign list has a tag filter (`?tag=`) and an Archived view (`?view=archived`). `bulk_update_campaigns` has two more operations: `archive` puts drafts and sent issues away from the list and takes a sent issue off the website's list; `unarchive` brings them back and lists the issue again if the archive was what unlisted it. Archiving deletes nothing, changes no sent email, and skips scheduled, sending, paused and held campaigns with a reason. Campaigns show `archived` in `get_campaign` and the list.
